# Plural in Serbian

## 1. Masculine gender

**Basic rule:** if a masculine noun ends in a consonant, in the plural it takes the ending **-и (-i)**.

### 1.1. Velar consonants: К (K), Г (G), Х (H)

If a masculine noun ends in the velar consonants **К (K), Г (G), Х (H)**, before the plural ending **-и (-i)** they always change into the sibilants **Ц (C), З (Z), С (S)**.

| Singular | Plural | Change |
|---|---|---|
| пољак (poljak) | пољаци (poljaci) | К (K) → Ц (C) |
| хирург (hirurg) | хирурзи (hirurzi) | Г (G) → З (Z) |
| монах (monah) | монаси (monasi) | Х (H) → С (S) |

### 1.2. Fleeting “А (A)”

**А (A)** drops out when an ending is added if the word contains a suffix such as **-ац (-ac)**, **-ак (-ak)**, or a combination such as **-ат (-at)**.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| нокат (nokat) | нокти (nokti) |
| старац (starac) | старци (starci) |

### 1.3. Fleeting “А (A)” + the change of “Л (L)” into “О (O)”

If historically the letter **Л (L)** stood at the end of a word or at the end of a syllable, over time it turned into **О (O)**, producing a masculine noun ending in **-о (-o)**. If, when the word changes, this letter again finds itself before a vowel, it “remembers” that it used to be the letter **Л (L)**.

**Example:** `котао (kotao) → котлови (kotlovi)`

1. Remove the fleeting **А (A)**: `котао (kotao) → кото (koto)`.
2. Add the ending **-ови (-ovi)**: `кото (koto) → котоови (kotoovi)`.
3. **О (O)** changes into **Л (L)**: `котоови (kotoovi) → котлови (kotlovi)`.

If the word clearly has the ending **-лац (-lac)** or **-лак (-lak)**, the letter **Л (L)**, on the contrary, changes into **О (O)**.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| заселак (zaselak) | засеоци (zaseoci) |
| читалац (čitalac) | читаоци (čitaoci) |

**How the change happens:**

1. Add the letter **-и (-i)** at the end: `читалаци (čitalaci)`, `заселаки (zaselaki)`.
2. The letter **А (A)** drops out: `читалци (čitalci)`, `заселки (zaselki)`.
3. **К (K)** changes into **Ц (C)**, or the already existing **Ц (C)** remains: `читалци (čitalci)`, `заселци (zaselci)`.
4. **Л (L)** changes into **О (O)**: `читаоци (čitaoci)`, `засеоци (zaseoci)`.

### 1.4. Dropping the suffix “-ИН (-IN)”

This rule concerns nationalities and inhabitants of cities/regions that in the singular end in **-ин (-in)**: `Србин (Srbin)`, `Русин (Rusin)` (= `Рус (Rus)`), `Београђанин (Beograđanin)`.

In the plural, this suffix is simply removed and **-и (-i)** is added.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| Србин (Srbin) | Срби (Srbi) |

### 1.5. Summary for masculine nouns

| Condition | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The word ends in **К (K), Г (G), Х (H)** | Change them into **Ц (C), З (Z), С (S)** | пољак (poljak) → пољаци (poljaci) |
| At the end of the word there is a suffix **-ац (-ac)**, **-ак (-ak)**, or a combination such as **-ат (-at)** | Remove **А (A)** | старац (starac) → старци (starci); нокат (nokat) → нокти (nokti) |
| The word ends in **-ао (-ao)** or **-ео (-eo)** | Restore the letter **Л (L)** and remove **А (A)** | котао (kotao) → котлови (kotlovi) |
| The word ends in the suffix **-лац (-lac)** | **А (A)** drops out, **Л (L)** changes into **О (O)** | читалац (čitalac) → читаоци (čitaoci) |
| The word denotes a nationality or an inhabitant and ends in **-ин (-in)** | Cut off **-ин (-in)** | Србин (Srbin) → Срби (Srbi) |

## 2. Masculine gender: monosyllabic words

**Short words often take long endings:** **-ови (-ovi)** / **-еви (-evi)**.

- The suffix **-еви (-evi)** is used after soft consonants: **ј (j), љ (lj), њ (nj), ч (č), ж (ž), ш (š), џ (dž), ђ (đ)**.
- Sometimes after **ц (c), з (z), с (s)**, **-еви (-evi)** is also used: `нос (nos) → носеви (nosevi)`, `стриц (stric) → стричеви (stričevi)`.
- But more often after **ц (c), з (z), с (s)**, **-ови (-ovi)** is still used: `појас (pojas) → појасови (pojasovi)`.
- The choice depends on the historical root of the word.
- The suffix **-ови (-ovi)** is used after hard consonants.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| син (sin) | синови (sinovi) |
| змај (zmaj) | змајеви (zmajevi) |

### 2.1. Monosyllabic words with a short ending

Certain semantic groups of monosyllabic words take the short ending.

| Group                     | Examples                                                                 |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Body parts                | зуб (zub) → зуби (zubi); прст (prst) → прсти (prsti)                     |
| Units of time/measurement | дан (dan) → дани (dani); сат (sat) → сати (sati)                         |
| Nationalities/people      | Грк (Grk) → Грци (Grci); ђак (đak) → ђаци (đaci); гост (gost) → гости (gosti) |
|                           |                                                                          |

### 2.2. Special cases

| Singular | Plural |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| брат (brat)        | браћа (braća)       |
| човек (čovek)      | људи (ljudi)        |
| господин (gospodin) | господа (gospoda)  |

If the root of a word ends in the letter **Ц (C)** and an ending beginning with the vowel **Е (E)** is attached to it, the letter **Ц (C)** changes into the palatal consonant **Ч (Č)**. If **Т (T)** or **Д (D)** stands before **Ч (Č), Џ (Dž), Ћ (Ć)**, or **Ђ (Đ)**, it disappears completely for ease of pronunciation.

| Singular | Plural |
| ------------------ | ------------------- |
| зец (zec)          | зечеви (zečevi)     |
| отац (otac)        | очеви (očevi)       |
| стриц (stric)      | стричеви (stričevi) |
| напитак (napitak)  | напици (napici)     |

There are, of course, other rules as well; it is not possible to list all of them here.

## 3. Neuter gender

**Basic rule:** if a neuter noun ends in **-о (-o)** or **-е (-e)**, in the plural it takes the ending **-а (-a)**.

| Type of ending | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| In **-о (-o)** | село (selo) | села (sela) |
| In **-о (-o)** | слово (slovo) | слова (slova) |
| In **-е (-e)** | море (more) | мора (mora) |
| In **-е (-e)** | поље (polje) | поља (polja) |

The ending **-е (-e)** in this case is caused by the softness of the preceding consonant.

### Special root-extension rules with insertions

### 3.1. Insertion “-ен- (-en-)”

Historically, these words ended in a nasal sound, which developed into **ЕН (EN)**.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| време (vreme) | времена (vremena) |
| племе (pleme) | племена (plemena) |

### 3.2. Insertion “-ет- (-et-)”

These are usually objects; such words often have Turkic origin.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| дугме (dugme) | дугмета (dugmeta) |
| парче (parče) | парчета (parčeta) |

### 3.3. Insertion “-ћи- (-ći-)”

Used for young animals and children.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| пиле (pile) | пилићи (pilići) |
| јаре (jare) | јарићи (jarići) |
| маче (mače) | мачићи (mačići) |

### 3.4. Insertion “-ес- (-es-)”

Used in poetic or elevated words.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| небо (nebo) | небеса (nebesa) |
| чудо (čudo) | чудеса (čudesa) |

### 3.5. Special case: a remnant of the dual number

A remnant of the dual number, which no longer exists, is preserved for paired things.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| око (oko) | очи (oči) |
| ухо (uho) | уши (uši) |

### 3.6. Special case

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| дете (dete) | деца (deca) |

## 4. Feminine gender

**Basic rule:** for feminine nouns ending in **-а (-a)**, the ending changes to **-е (-e)**.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| жена (žena) | жене (žene) |
| кућа (kuća) | куће (kuće) |
| лутка (lutka) | лутке (lutke) |

### 4.1. Feminine nouns ending in a consonant

If a feminine noun ends in a consonant, the ending **-и (-i)** is added.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| ствар (stvar) | ствари (stvari) |
| кост (kost) | кости (kosti) |
| реч (reč) | речи (reči) |

### 4.2. Feminine nouns ending in the vowel “-о (-o)”

If a feminine noun ends in the vowel **-о (-o)**, the ending changes to a form with **-ли (-li)** according to the rule “Fleeting **А (A)** + the change of **Л (L)** into **О (O)**”.

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| мисао (misao) | мисли (misli) |
| со (so) | соли (soli) |

### 4.3. Special cases due to different meanings

| Singular | Plural | Meaning                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| дрво (drvo)        | дрва (drva)         | firewood                        |
| дрво (drvo)        | дрвеће (drveće)     | trees as vegetation             |
| дрво (drvo)        | дрвета (drveta)     | individual trees                |
| лист (list)        | листови (listovi)   | sheets, pages, individual leaves |
| лист (list)        | лишће (lišće)       | foliage                         |

## 5. Plural-only words

These words do not change by number because they are used only in the plural.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| панталоне (pantalone) | trousers / pants |
| маказе (makaze) | scissors |
| наочаре (naočare) | glasses |
| санке (sanke) | sled / sleigh |
| мердевине (merdevine) | stepladder / ladder |
| врата (vrata) | door / doors |
| уста (usta) | mouth |

### Agreement

- If a plural-only word ends in **-е (-e)**, treat it like a group of women: `оне су... (one su...)`, `ове маказе... (ove makaze...)`.
- If a plural-only word ends in **-а (-a)**, treat it like a group of villages/windows: `она су... (ona su...)`, `ова врата... (ova vrata...)`.
